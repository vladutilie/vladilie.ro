## Context

BookTruck is the mobile library of [Cărțile pe Față](https://cartilepefata.ro), the reading association whose IT infrastructure I've looked after pro-bono for years. The trucks lend books to children in rural schools, so behind every trip there is stock to track, loans to record, returns to chase — and a lot of photos and videos.

Lumea BookTruck is the internal app that holds all of that together. The public side is a searchable catalogue of the books that travel with the trucks; the panel is where librarians and volunteers do their day-to-day work.

## The problem

Two things were scattered. The catalogue, stock and loans to schools lived in a mix of spreadsheets and memory. And the media from each trip ended up, in the association's own words, chaotically in a WhatsApp group — compressed, unsorted and impossible to find a month later, even though the organisation already had plenty of Google Drive space.

## What I built

- **A public catalogue**: book search, catalogue stats, the latest books, the most borrowed titles overall and per month, and a page for every book.
- **The panel**: books, authors, genres, schools and locations; stock and stock movements; loans and returns through a quick transaction modal.
- **Fast book entry**: ISBN barcode scanning right in the browser (including iPhones with several cameras), automatic metadata lookup by ISBN, and processed covers with blurhash placeholders.
- **Volunteer upload sessions**: an admin creates a session with a label and an expiry date. That automatically creates a subfolder in the association's Shared Drive and a long random link. Volunteers open the link, optionally type their name and upload — no account needed. Admins can close or extend a session at any time.
- **A team area**: email invitations, roles, banning, a sign-in and impersonation log, and live presence so you can see who else is in the panel.

Permissions are a matrix per resource (books, stock, loans, returns, schools, stats, team…) for three roles: admin, librarian and volunteer.

## Under the hood

The interesting decision is in the uploads. Files **never pass through the server**. The server only opens a Google Drive *resumable upload* session with a service account and hands its URI to the browser, which then sends the file in 8 MB chunks with `Content-Range`, retrying each chunk on its own. Proxying the files would have hit the 4.5 MB request-body limit and the timeouts of Vercel Functions; this way a 2 GB video from a school courtyard is just as safe as a photo. Volunteers never get browse access to the Drive, and each completed file is logged (size, type, duration), which feeds the dashboard widgets.

Authentication moved from Supabase Auth to Better Auth with passwordless magic links and sign-up disabled. I kept every existing user id and foreign key with an additive backfill, so nothing had to be remapped. Magic links are rate limited in Redis per email and per IP — every email costs credits — and the limiter fails open if Redis is down. Redis also caches the public stats and carries the presence heartbeats.

Schema changes are hand-written SQL migrations, and every date is pinned to Europe/Bucharest so the server and the browser always show the same time. The core rules — stock movements, loans, invitations, logs — are covered by Vitest.

## Result

One link per trip, and files land organised in Drive instead of a chat group. Large videos upload reliably from a phone without ever touching — or overloading — the server. Librarians and volunteers share one panel, each seeing only what their role allows, and the catalogue the children borrow from is finally searchable by anyone.
